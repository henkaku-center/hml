That inversion **is** the computational theory of social cognition: *action understanding as inverse planning* = Bayesian inversion of a rational (MDP/POMDP) planner, `P(goal | actions) ∝ P(actions | goal) · P(goal)`, where the likelihood is exactly last week's softmax MDP policy run in reverse.

## Area 1 — Inverse planning / goal inference (the core, HIGH confidence)

- **Baker, Saxe & Tenenbaum (2009), *Action understanding as inverse planning*, Cognition 113(3):329–349.** The canonical paper. Formalizes mental-state inference as "inverting a model of rational planning using Bayesian inference," and states action understanding "can be characterized as a kind of inverse planning or **inverse reinforcement learning** (Ng & Russell 2000)," the action-side analogue of **vision-as-inverse-graphics**. *"Inverse planning is simply running the principle of rationality in reverse."* (Conf. precursor: Baker, Tenenbaum & Saxe 2007, *Goal inference as inverse planning*, CogSci — the assigned reading.)
  - **Teachable numbers (Experiment 1, the ONLINE goal-inference task — label it as Exp. 1, not "across experiments"):** cross-validated correlation with human frame-by-frame goal judgments — **changing-goal model r = 0.97** (best), subgoals r = 0.93, low-level motion heuristic r = 0.96, single-fixed-goal r = 0.82. The headline: modeling that *goals can change* fits people best. (Parenthetical values in the original table are SDs.)
  - **The demo paradigm = Widget A.** Agent moves through a maze toward one of several marked goals; freeze the clip partway; ask "where is it headed?"; the model's posterior over goals updates frame by frame. This *is* the "freeze-the-animation" interaction.
- **Two misconceptions to teach head-on (both are real properties of the model):**
  1. **The inversion is ill-posed.** Many goal/reward combinations explain the same few steps (walking toward the door fits "leave," "answer it," "block it"). BSТ 2009: *"the inverse problem is ill-posed; its solution requires strong prior knowledge."* The IRL primer (Gleave & Toyer 2022, arXiv:2203.11409) independently: "many different reward functions are consistent with the observed expert behaviour." → **Bayes resolves it by weighting multiple explanations by fit; the prior + the rationality assumption do the disambiguating.** Don't let students think the answer is unique — teach that it's a posterior.
  2. **"The prior does the work" / rationality assumption.** The likelihood `P(actions | goal)` comes from assuming the agent is a (noisily) rational planner — a **softmax / Boltzmann** policy with temperature β. If the agent blunders, the model rationalizes by inferring a *weirder goal* (or an unseen obstacle to explain a detour). "Assume rationality, blame the goal" is the mechanism, not a bug (slack prompt 2).
- **Naive Utility Calculus** (Jara-Ettinger, Gweon, Schulz & Tenenbaum 2016, *TiCS* 20:589–604; CogSci 2015). People assume agents "choose goals and actions to maximize **rewards** expected minus **costs** expected," formalized as `p(C,R | Actions) ∝ p(Actions | C,R) · p(C,R)` — a generative MDP-like model run backwards. Present from infancy through adulthood; unifies inferences about preferences, competence, and moral character. **This is the bridge from the cog-sci ToM material to the machine-IRL reward-recovery material** (recover cost *and* reward = Widget D).
- **Jara-Ettinger (2019), *Theory of Mind as Inverse Reinforcement Learning*, Curr. Opin. Behav. Sci. 29:105–110.** A recent **review** that crystallizes the name and the one-liner: "mental-state inference from observable behavior is equivalent to inverse reinforcement learning: inferring an agent's unobservable model of the world and reward function, given observed actions." Forward = simulate the RL model; inference = invert it. ⚠️ **Attribution (per instructor — fact-checked):** the *framework itself* is **Baker & Tenenbaum's** (Baker, Tenenbaum & Saxe 2007; Baker, Saxe & Tenenbaum 2009, above — they coin the inverse-planning / inverse-RL characterization). Cite Jara-Ettinger 2019 as the modern **synthesis/review**, *not* the originator; **credit Baker & Tenenbaum for the framework.**

## Area 1b — Beliefs, not just goals: Bayesian Theory of Mind (the POMDP hinge, HIGH confidence)

- **Baker, Jara-Ettinger, Saxe & Tenenbaum (2017), Nature Human Behaviour 1:0064.** ⚠️ **Title correction:** the published title is *"Rational quantitative attribution of beliefs, desires **and percepts** in human mentalizing"* — NOT "…and intentions." Present BDI as *your* framing, not a quote.
  - BToM "probabilistically inverts AI approaches to rational planning and state estimation, using **partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs)**": `Pr(B,D,P,S | A) ∝ Pr(A | B,D)·Pr(B | P,B₀)·Pr(P | S)·Pr(B₀,D,S)`. Three causal factors: **perception** (line-of-sight / isovist, `Pr(P|S)`), **Bayesian belief update** (`Pr(B|P,B₀)`), **softmax-rational planning** (`Pr(A|B,D)`, parameter β). Belief is both an *effect* of percepts+priors and a *cause* of action.
  - **The food-truck demo (= the canonical "why beliefs" example, Widget/figure ★).** ~5×10 gridworld with a **building occluding part of the scene**; three cuisines **Korean / Lebanese / Mexican** across two spots; the agent is unsure which trucks are present today. Diagnostic path: the agent **walks around the building to check the far spot, doesn't find its favorite, returns to a nearer, less-preferred truck.** Observers jointly infer a **belief and a desire — including a desire for a truck that isn't even in the scene.** Impossible for shortest-path goal accounts. → motivates the entire POMDP block.
  - **The 2009→2017 move IS the lecture's middle hinge:** 2009 inverted an **MDP** to infer goals (assuming correct beliefs); 2017 upgrades **MDP → POMDP**, making the agent's *belief* part of the hidden state to infer. *The belief representation a POMDP planner uses to ACT is exactly what an observer inverts to READ A MIND.*

## Area 2 — Modern developments (the "refresh the stale tail")

### IRL methods (cite, one line each)
- **MaxEnt IRL** (Ziebart, Maas, Bagnell & Dey 2008, AAAI) / **Maximum Causal Entropy** (Ziebart 2010, CMU thesis): resolve ill-posedness with the max-entropy principle — among reward-consistent trajectory distributions pick the least-committal, giving `P(trajectory) ∝ exp(reward)`, the soft-optimal/Boltzmann demonstrator; reward fit by MLE. **This is the same softmax used as the inverse-planning likelihood** — the seam joining cog-sci ToM to machine IRL, and the path to deep IRL.
- **GAIL** (Ho & Ermon 2016, NeurIPS, arXiv:1606.03476): imitation as a GAN — a discriminator separates expert vs. learner state-action pairs; the policy matches the expert's occupancy measure. Skips explicit reward recovery (discriminator ≠ transferable reward).
- **AIRL** (Fu, Luo & Levine 2018, ICLR, arXiv:1710.11248): structures the discriminator so a **transferable, disentangled reward** falls out — robust to changed dynamics.
- **Honest state of IRL:** fundamentally **underconstrained** — demonstrations don't uniquely identify a reward (constant shifts, potential-based shaping all explain the same behavior). MaxEnt's entropy prior and AIRL's disentanglement are *regularizers* that pick *a* reward, not *the* reward. Treat any recovered reward as one consistent explanation among many — and sensitive to the assumed rationality model.

### IRL → alignment (the contemporary payoff, HIGH confidence)
- **RLHF and DPO are both IRL** (Sun & van der Schaar 2025, arXiv:2507.13158, survey with a section literally titled "Practical IRL via Reward Modeling"): RLHF fits a **reward model `r_θ(x,y)→ℝ`** to human **preference comparisons** (Bradley–Terry choice model — a MaxEnt-style model), **freezes** it, then optimizes the policy via **PPO**. This is reward learning from human behavior = IRL's inverse problem. Distinction to flag: classic IRL uses *demonstrations*, RLHF uses *preferences* → RLHF is **preference-based IRL**. **DPO** (Rafailov et al. 2023, NeurIPS, arXiv:2305.18290) collapses the two stages: the optimal policy implies an **implicit reward** `r̂(x,y)=β·log[π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x)]`, so you optimize preferences directly — "the reward lives inside the policy."
  - **Callback to Week 8:** reward hacking / positive cycles (the GardenPath `af` loop) is the *same bug* at frontier scale — why reward models are hard to get right. Plants the Weeks 11–13 alignment thread.
- **Cooperative IRL / assistance games** (Hadfield-Menell, Russell, Abbeel & Dragan 2016, NeurIPS, arXiv:1606.03137): a **two-player cooperative partial-information game** — both rewarded by the *human's* reward, but only the human knows it. Unlike classic IRL (isolated optimal demonstrator), optimal CIRL produces **active teaching, active learning, and communicative actions**. **Theorem:** efficient expert demonstration is provably **suboptimal** (`br(br(π_E)) ≠ π_E`) — you should *deviate* from efficient behavior to communicate the objective. **CIRL reduces to a POMDP** — the clean bridge from inverse planning → alignment AND → the teaching/legibility theme AND → POMDP solvers, all at once.

### Machine Theory of Mind
- **ToMnet** (Rabinowitz et al. 2018, ICML, arXiv:1802.07740, DeepMind): a **meta-learning "observer" network** that watches agents and predicts their behavior/mental states from behavior alone; learns a population prior, then bootstraps from a few observations of a new agent (amortized inference); **passes a Sally-Anne-style false-belief test** in gridworlds. **The contrast to draw:** ToMnet is the **learned/amortized** cousin of **Bayesian inverse-planning ToM** — same inverse problem, but a neural net learns the mapping instead of explicitly inverting a hand-specified planner. The *amortized vs. Bayesian* axis parallels the LLM-ToM mechanism question. (Successors for a footnote: ToM2C, ToMnet-G, ToMCAT 2025; review Cuzzolin et al. arXiv:2303.11594.)

### LLM Theory of Mind — the contested capstone (⚠️ GENUINELY UNSETTLED — teach as a live debate)
The honest structure is **claim → rebuttal → reply**, still ongoing in 2025–26. Present both poles; the unifying through-line is **behavioral pass ≠ mechanism**.
- **Pro-capability:** **Kosinski 2024** (*PNAS* 121(45):e2405460121; preprint 2023 arXiv:2302.02083) — 11 LLMs on 40 false-belief tasks / 640 prompts with matched true-belief + reversed controls; **GPT-4 solved 75% — the level of 6-year-olds**; claims ToM-like ability **"may have spontaneously emerged"** as a byproduct of language (note the hedge — don't strawman as "GPT has ToM"). **Strachan et al. 2024** (*Nat. Hum. Behav.* 8(7):1285–1295) — GPT-4/3.5/LLaMA2 vs. **1,907 humans** across false belief, irony, faux pas, hinting, strange stories; **GPT-4 at/above human on false belief, irony, hinting, strange stories**. **Street et al. 2024/2026** (arXiv:2405.18870 / *Front. Hum. Neurosci.* doi:10.3389/fnhum.2025.1633272) — **GPT-4 reaches adult level on higher-order recursive ToM, exceeds adults at 6th order.**
- **Skeptical:** **Ullman 2023** (arXiv:2302.08399) — **trivial, ToM-irrelevant perturbations that preserve belief structure flip success to failure** (transparent container, uninformative label, trusted testimony → model still says the agent holds the false belief, ~95–99%). Thesis: *"outlying failure cases should outweigh average success rates"* (a system that fails 213×261 hasn't learned multiplication). **Strachan's faux-pas result** — GPT-4 *underperformed* humans; LLaMA2's apparent "win" was a **response bias** (defaulting to "attribute ignorance"), and GPT-4's failure was **hyperconservatism** ("insufficient information") not inability. **Pang et al. 2025** (*PNAS* doi:10.1073/pnas.2507080122) — invokes **Morgan's Canon**: attributing ToM is premature until memorized-template / data-contamination explanations are ruled out. **Kosinski's reply** (doi:10.1073/pnas.2511485122) — a single belief-tracking ability is more parsimonious. **Unresolved — cite both.**
- **Slide bottom line (4 sentences, instructor's lean: SKEPTICAL):** Modern LLMs pass many classic false-belief, irony, hinting, and even higher-order ToM vignettes at or above adult-human levels — the positive results are real. But the same models **fail on minimal, logically-irrelevant perturbations** that preserve belief structure and on faux-pas detection, and apparent "wins" come from response biases (attributing ignorance) or hyperconservatism rather than inference. A **behavioral pass is not evidence of the mechanism**, and the results are equally consistent with memorized ToM-shaped templates / training-data contamination, which cannot currently be ruled out. **The defensible 2026 position: treat current LLM "theory of mind" as unproven — impressive ToM-shaped pattern-matching, not demonstrated mental-state representation.** (Present the capability results, then dismantle them; don't strawman Kosinski's hedged "may have emerged.")
- **Data caveat:** if putting Strachan per-test percentages on a slide, pull them from the paper's figures directly.

## Area 3 — POMDP pedagogy (the Tiger problem + teaching sequence)

- **The Tiger problem** (Kaelbling, Littman & Cassandra 1998, *AI* 101:99–134; conf. Cassandra et al. AAAI 1994). The canonical 2-state POMDP — small enough to draw the whole belief space as a line.
  - **States** (2): tiger-left, tiger-right. **Actions** (3): listen, open-left, open-right. **Observations** (2): hear-left, hear-right.
  - **Rewards (canonical, verified across pomdp-py / R `pomdp` / JuliaPOMDP):** listen **−1**, open correct door **+10**, open tiger door **−100**.
  - **Observation model:** after *listen*, hear the correct side with prob **0.85** (wrong 0.15); after opening, uninformative and state resets to uniform.
  - **Discount — SOURCES DISAGREE (flag on slide):** **γ = 0.95** (KLC/pomdp-py canonical, use this) vs. 0.75 (R `pomdp` vignette). Different γ → different alpha-vector count and exact threshold; cite the γ on any solved-policy slide.
  - **Belief update (verified by Bayes at 0.85):** belief = one number `b = P(tiger-left)`; each consistent *listen* multiplies the odds by `0.85/0.15 ≈ 5.67`. Starting at **0.5**, hearing left each time: **0.5 → 0.85 → 0.9698 → 0.9945**. A hear-left then hear-right cancels exactly back to 0.5 (nice widget beat). These match JuliaPOMDP's Tiger notebook printout. **Threshold rule:** keep listening in the uncertain middle; open once belief crosses ~0.97. **One growl (b=0.85) is NOT enough — you listen again; two agreeing growls and you open.** → **Poll 2.**
  - **The α-vector / PWLC picture (the figure to put on a slide):** with 2 states, plot value `V(b)` vs `b∈[0,1]` (the 1-D belief simplex). Each action's expected value is a **straight line** (an alpha-vector); the optimal value function is the **upper envelope (piecewise-linear convex)**; the regions where each line wins = the optimal action (listen near 0.5, open near the edges); breakpoints = the belief thresholds. This single plot makes "the policy is a function of belief" visually obvious.
- **Belief state → belief MDP (the conceptual spine):** the agent never sees `s`, only `o`; it maintains a **belief** `b(s)=P(s | history)`, updated by Bayes after each (a,o): `b'(s') ∝ P(o|s',a) Σ_s P(s'|s,a) b(s)`. The belief is a **sufficient statistic for history**, so the POMDP becomes a fully-observable **MDP over belief states**. Planning in a POMDP = solving an MDP on the belief simplex. For 2 states the belief is a point on a line — *the* pedagogical gift of Tiger.
- **Teaching sequence — Kochenderfer, Wheeler & Wray (2022), *Algorithms for Decision Making* (free, algorithmsbook.com), Part IV "State Uncertainty," Ch 19–23**, the textbook for **Stanford AA228/CS238**:
  - **Ch 19 Beliefs** — discrete filter, Kalman/EKF/UKF, **particle filter**.
  - **Ch 20 Exact Belief-State Planning** — belief-MDP, **alpha-vectors**, value iteration (the PWLC picture).
  - **Ch 21 Offline Approx.** — **QMDP**, Fast Informed Bound, **point-based VI (PBVI)**, **SARSOP**.
  - **Ch 22 Online** — lookahead+rollouts, **POMCP** (MCTS over histories), **DESPOT** (determinized scenarios).
  - **Ch 23 Controllers** — finite-state controllers.
  - **Mirror this arc (beliefs → exact → offline → online) compressed to ~1 slide of taxonomy.** Solver one-liners: **QMDP** = solve the underlying MDP, act greedily on belief, ignore info-gathering value (cheap); **PBVI/SARSOP** = track alpha-vectors only at sampled reachable beliefs; **POMCP/DESPOT** = online tree search over action-observation histories with state particles.
- **POMDPs.jl / JuliaPOMDP** — `POMDPModels.jl` ships `TigerPOMDP`; belief = a length-2 `DiscreteBelief` vector printing `[0.5,0.5]→[0.15,0.85]→[0.0302,0.9698]`. The **gallery** mostly shows agent-in-environment GIFs with belief as a **particle cloud overlaid on the map** (EscapeRoomba, LaserTag) — there is *no* shipped animated belief-bar/simplex plot, so **we build that ourselves** (Widget B). Particle-cloud-contracting is the idiom for the gridworld-belief-heatmap variant.

## Area 4 — Teaching as inverse planning & legibility (flip the inversion)

The "flip the flip" (slack prompt 4b): if observers infer goals from actions, an agent can *choose* actions that make its goal **legible** — that's teaching.
- **Ho, Littman, MacGlashan, Cushman & Austerweil (2016), *Showing versus doing: teaching by demonstration*, NeurIPS** (instructor's own work; code: github.com/markkho/demonstration-teach-learn). Gridworld (blue agent, yellow goal, red hazards): people **doing** a task vs. **showing** it move *differently* — demonstrators deviate from merely-optimal paths to make the reward **unambiguous**. Bayesian model: the teacher picks demonstrations that maximize the observer's posterior on the correct reward. = **Widget C**, gridworld-native.
- **Ho, Littman, Cushman & Austerweil (2015), *Teaching with rewards and punishments: reinforcement or communication?*, CogSci.** Reward as a **signal** vs. as a **reinforcer**. ⚠️ **The RL assignment's GardenPath world is from this paper** (`\citep{ho15}` in rl.tex) — doubly on-topic; the Week-8 `af` feedback scheme is the "communication" reading of reward.
- **Ho, Littman & Austerweil (2017), *Teaching by intervention*, CogSci.** Correction-as-signal — extends teaching-as-inverse-planning into live intervention.
- **Dragan, Lee & Srinivasa (2013), *Legibility and Predictability of Robot Motion*, HRI** (CMU). The canonical reach-to-one-of-two-objects figure: **predictable/efficient** path hugs the ambiguous midline and resolves late; **legible** path **veers conspicuously toward the true goal early** (longer, but the observer infers the goal sooner). Formalized as two inferences in **opposite directions** — *predictability* = `P(trajectory | goal)` (goal→traj), *legibility* scored by the observer's `P(goal | trajectory-so-far)` weighted to early timesteps (traj→goal). **Explicitly analogizes to action interpretation in psychology** — the direct bridge to ToM. = **Widget C** geometry.
- **Shafto, Goodman & Griffiths (2014), *A rational account of pedagogical reasoning*, Cognitive Psychology** (web.stanford.edu/~ngoodman/papers/shaftogg14.pdf). **Pedagogical sampling:** a teacher chooses data to maximize the learner's belief in the correct hypothesis, and the learner knows this — recursive cooperative inference. The communication-theoretic generalization of teaching-as-inverse-planning.
- **Unifying claim (supported by the verified CIRL result):** legible/communicative action provably beats efficient demonstration when the point is to convey an objective. *Acting and communicating are the same operation viewed from two directions.*
