To date, we’ve focused on probabilistic models where we can specify the family of distributions that our parameters and data come from. For the remainder of the semester, we will venture into nonparametric or practically nonparametric models (artificial neural networks). To setup this transition, we’re first going to discuss a famous dilemma in statistical modeling – the bias-variance dilemma. We’ll then introduce nonparametric estimation very briefly and the Bayesian form of it. We’ll cover Bayesian nonparametric mixture modeling, which is one of the most common uses. Then I’ll briefly cover Gaussian processes so you have some exposure to them. They are most often used for reaction-time modeling as they are used in the drift-diffusion modeling framework pioneered by Ratcliff.
