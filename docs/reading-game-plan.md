# How readings, presentations, and discussion posts work

**Human and Machine Learning, SP26 — Chiba Tech SDS**

This is your pocket guide for the semester. Three things happen every week, and there's one thing that happens once.

---

## 1. Read before class

**Where to find the readings:** the password-protected course site at `https://hml.chibatech.dev/readings.html` (password on Slack, pinned in `#hml-2026`).

**What's posted per week:**
- **Required readings** — short, usually 1 textbook chapter, must be done before Friday.
- **Optional readings** — longer follow-ups; read if interested.
- **Presentation candidates** — 2–4 papers, one of which a student will present (starting Week 4). You don't need to read all of these; you do need to read the one that gets chosen for your week.

**How to read a paper:**
1. Read the abstract, then skip to the figures. Look at every figure. If you understand the figures, you mostly understand the paper.
2. Read the introduction and the discussion. Skip the middle of the methods on a first pass.
3. Come to class with **one concrete question** about the paper. Write it down. You'll need it for Step 3 below.

**Time budget:** plan 2–4 hours per week for reading.

---

## 2. Sign up for your paper presentation (ONCE, before Week 4)

**What this is:** each student presents one paper for the class, during one of Weeks 4–12. The presentation is ~15 minutes, counts for 7.5% of your grade, and uses the rubric in `resources/classPresentationGuidelines.pdf`.

**How signup works:**

1. In Week 2 we'll hand you a signup sheet with the 9 available weeks (Week 4 through Week 12). Put your initials next to your top-3 preferred weeks.

2. By **Wednesday of Week 3** (May 13, 2026), open the `#paper-signups` Slack channel. Prof. Austerweil will have posted a thread for each of the 9 weeks. For your preferred week, reply with:
   - your name
   - **your top-3 papers** from that week's `presentation_candidates` list, ranked
   - one sentence on why you want to present this paper

3. By **Friday of Week 3** (May 15), we'll publish the final assignments. You'll have **4 weeks** between assignment and presentation to read, meet with Prof. Austerweil in office hours, and prepare.

**If more than one student wants the same slot:** the person who puts it higher in their top-3 wins; ties broken by earliest Slack post.

**If you don't sign up:** Prof. Austerweil will assign you a week. You won't like this as much as picking.

---

## 3. Post a discussion contribution on Slack, each week

**Where:** `#weekly-discussion` channel on the class Slack. There's one pinned thread per week.

**When:** by **9:00 PM Thursday** (the night before class).

**What to post (pick one):**
- A question about this week's required reading that you genuinely want answered in class, OR
- A connection you noticed between this week's reading and something from an earlier week (or another class), OR
- A prediction about what this week's lecture will cover, based on the reading.

**How long:** 2–4 sentences. Quality over volume. One thoughtful question beats a paragraph of generalities.

**Grading:** 12.5% of your grade is for reflections — you need 6 of 12 weeks posted to get full credit. You can skip any 6 weeks you want.

**Why we do it this way:** your post tells Prof. Austerweil what to spend extra time on in class. Students who post by Thursday reliably get their questions addressed on Friday.

## The rhythm of a week (cheat sheet)

| Day | You do |
|-----|--------|
| **Monday–Wednesday** | Read the required reading for this week. Also glance at next week's required reading if you're ambitious. |

| **Friday in class** | Show up. Bring your question. If it's your presentation week, present. |
| **Friday after class** | (Optional) post a one-line reflection in the weekly thread if something clicked. |

## Troubleshooting

- **"I can't access the password-protected site."** Check Slack `#hml-2026` for the current password. If you can't find it, DM Prof. Austerweil.
- **"I didn't get my top choice for presentations."** The second-choice slot is usually just as good. If you have a strong reason (conflict, prior expertise), DM Prof. Austerweil in the first 48 hours.
- **"I missed a reading because the class before me ran over."** One missed reading is fine. More than one week in a row, come to office hours so we can get you caught up.
- **"I want to present a paper not on the candidate list."** DM Prof. Austerweil your pitch. If it's on-theme for the week and has a plausible connection to the lecture, it's usually a yes.
