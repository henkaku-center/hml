Readings & Discussion Game Plan

How readings, Slack signups, and discussion posts fit together.

Human and Machine Learning, SP26 — Chiba Tech SDS

This is your pocket guide for the semester. Three things happen every week, and there's one thing that happens once.

1. Read before class

Where to find the readings: the password-protected course site at https://hml.chibatech.dev/readings.html (password on Slack, pinned in #hml-2026).

What's posted per week: - Required readings — short, usually 1 textbook chapter, must be done before Friday. - Optional readings — longer follow-ups; read if interested. - Presentation candidates — 2–4 papers, one of which a student may present (starting Week 4). Some weeks will have no presenter; those weeks you can skip the candidates entirely.

How to read a paper: 1. Read the abstract, then skip to the figures. Look at every figure. If you understand the figures, you mostly understand the paper. 2. Read the introduction and the discussion. Skip the middle of the methods on a first pass. 3. Come to class with one concrete question about the paper. Write it down. You'll need it for Step 3 below.

Time budget: plan 2–4 hours per week for reading.

2. Sign up for your paper presentation (ONCE, before Week 4)

What this is: each student presents one paper for the class, during one of Weeks 4–12. The presentation is ~15 minutes, counts for 7.5% of your grade, and uses the rubric in the Presentation guidelines.

Class size: 4 students, 9 candidate weeks (Week 4 through Week 12). Not every week will have a presenter — that's fine. Pick a week that interests you.

How signup works:

  1. By Wed May 13, 2026 (Week 3), DM Prof. Austerweil on Slack with your top-3 papers, ranked. The three can come from any of Weeks 4–12 — they don't have to be from the same week. Pick papers you're genuinely curious about. No justification needed — just the ranked list.

  2. By Fri May 15 (end of Week 3), final presentation assignments are published on the readings page. You'll have 4+ weeks between your assignment and your presentation to read carefully and prepare.

If two students' top choices overlap: whoever DMs first gets priority; the other falls to their next preference.

If you want to present a paper not on any candidate list: DM Prof. Austerweil your pitch. If it's on-theme for some week and has a plausible connection to that lecture, it's usually a yes.

Meeting before you present: reach out on Slack to schedule a 1-on-1 meeting with Prof. Austerweil the week before your presentation. The meeting is highly recommended — papers have more in them than you can cover in 15 minutes, and we'll decide together which parts to focus on.

3. Post a discussion contribution on Slack, each week

Where: the #hml-2026 Slack channel. On Monday of each week, Prof. Austerweil will post a thread for the upcoming Friday's readings. Reply to that thread.

When: by class on Friday. Posting by Thursday is strongly preferred — earlier posts are more likely to shape what the lecture spends extra time on.

What to post (pick one): - A question about this week's required reading that you genuinely want answered in class, OR - A connection you noticed between this week's reading and something from an earlier week (or another class), OR - A prediction about what this week's lecture will cover, based on the reading.

How long: 150–200 words. Quality over volume — one thoughtful question beats a paragraph of generalities.

Grading: pass/fail. Posts together count for 12.5% of your grade; you need 5 of 11 eligible weeks posted for full credit (Weeks 2–12; Week 1 was the intro session and has no post). You can skip any 6 of those 11 weeks you want.

Why we do it this way: your post tells Prof. Austerweil what to spend extra time on in class. Students who post by Thursday reliably get their questions addressed on Friday.

The rhythm of a week (cheat sheet)

Day What happens
Monday Prof. Austerweil posts the weekly discussion thread in #hml-2026.

Troubleshooting

  • "I can't access the password-protected site." Check Slack #hml-2026 for the current password. If you can't find it, DM Prof. Austerweil.
  • "I didn't get my top choice for presentations." DM Prof. Austerweil. The second-choice slot is usually just as good, and some weeks have no presenter, so there's usually flexibility.
  • "I missed a reading because the week before ran over." One missed reading is fine. If you fall behind two weeks in a row, DM Prof. Austerweil to schedule a meeting so we can catch you up.
  • "I want to present a paper not on the candidate list." DM Prof. Austerweil your pitch. On-theme + plausible connection to the lecture = usually yes.
